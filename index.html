About this book

A synthetic stellar spectrum is the bridge between a star's physical parameters and the light we record. Production codes that compute it — Kurucz's ATLAS12 and SYNTHE, and their modern Python reimplementation pykurucz — are decades of hardened physics wrapped in performance machinery. This book strips that machinery away and rebuilds the physics one layer at a time, in code short enough to read in a sitting.

The discipline that keeps it honest: at the end of every step we import the corresponding pykurucz routine and check that our from-scratch number matches it. The arc starts with the Sun over a narrow window of atomic lines, assembles a full emergent spectrum, then closes the loop by building the model atmosphere itself, and finally adds molecules. By the end you will have reproduced a real stellar spectrum, line by line, and understood every term that went into it.
